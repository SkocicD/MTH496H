\section{Triangulations}
The next geometric tool to study to understand the crust is the triangulation.
Once again, triangulations are used across the field of discrete geometry as a building block in many algorithms.
A triangulations essentially takes a set of points and converts it to a triangular mesh, providing it with a sort of structure.
As rest of this section will show, there are usually many different ways to triangulate a set of points, and the last part of this section will do a deep dive into a specific kind of triangulation, the Delaunay triangulation.
For the crust, a subset of the edges of this Delaunay triangulation are chosen to reconstruct the curve.
Before exploring this special triangulation, the basics of triangulations are explored, starting with the simple case of the triangulation of polygons, then moving on to triangulating an unlabeled set of points, then finally proving some interesting results about the Delaunay triangulation.
